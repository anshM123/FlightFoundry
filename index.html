02 — Evidence

Failures are unfinished experiments.

A flight is not a single event. Open it and time becomes an axis: sensor returns, confidence, the estimator's belief, the planner's commitments, the control effort that ran out of authority. The incident stops being a story and becomes data.

Discover

Find where autonomy breaks.

FlightFoundry searches simulation and accumulated operational data for failure modes, edge cases and regions where performance is fragile. Rather than waiting for engineers to stumble into failures, the system goes looking for them.

    Isolate

    Turn messy failures into useful counterexamples.

    A real flight contains thousands of simultaneous variables. FlightFoundry searches for the smallest scenario that still preserves the mechanism — relaxing every condition that was never the cause, and keeping only what the failure actually needs.

    Diagnose

    Find the mechanism, not just the anomaly.

    Evidence is connected across logs, simulation state, perception, planning, control, sensors and operating conditions until one chain explains the outcome. The result is not an alert. It is a diagnosis with timestamps.

      Repair

      Turn counterexamples into corrective experience.

      The minimal counterexample becomes the centre of a new distribution: variations in lighting, angle, wind, geometry and closing speed. Relevant prior experience is retrieved, new scenarios are generated, and targeted model, data, parameter or system changes are proposed.

      Verify

      The system improving autonomy shouldn't grade its own homework.

      Improvement and evaluation are separated. Candidate versions are run against protected scenarios the repair process cannot see, and compared against the version currently deployed. A candidate must demonstrate the intended improvement without unacceptable regressions anywhere else in the envelope.

      Improvement
      verified.

      Research — SEAL

      What happens when a robot learns from its own exam?

      A continually improving system that is repeatedly measured against the same protected evaluation will, given enough attempts, begin to learn the evaluation itself. SEAL is FlightFoundry's research direction on that problem: how much information may leave a protected evaluator before the evaluation stops meaning anything.

      Active research direction. No results are claimed here.

      The loop

      Ship improvement, not hope.

      Every deployment produces evidence. Every failure becomes a counterexample. Every counterexample becomes an experiment. Every experiment can produce a repair. Every repair faces independent evaluation. Then the aircraft flies again.

      What it is

      FlightFoundry is not a fleet dashboard, a mission planner, an observability tool, a data generator or another simulator. It sits above all of them.

      It is the improvement infrastructure connecting failures, simulations, data, models, autonomy software, evaluation and deployment — effectively a CI/CD system for physical autonomy. Instead of asking whether the software compiles, it asks whether the aircraft actually became better.

      Its advantage compounds. Across aircraft, sensors, environments, autonomy stacks and missions, FlightFoundry can accumulate knowledge about recurring failure mechanisms and the kinds of corrective experience that actually resolve them — a failure-to-repair intelligence layer no single fleet can build alone.
