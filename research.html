Research — SEAL

What happens when a robot learns from its own exam?

FlightFoundry's protected evaluation layer is only meaningful for as long as the repair process cannot learn it. SEAL is the research direction on exactly that problem.

01

The tension

A continually improving system needs feedback from evaluation to improve. But a system that is repeatedly measured against the same reusable evaluation will, given enough attempts, begin to learn the evaluation itself rather than the capability the evaluation was standing in for.

Feedback must be useful enough to improve the system, and limited enough to preserve the integrity of the evaluation.

Both ends of that statement are easy on their own. Unlimited feedback produces a repair loop that optimises against a fixed exam. No feedback produces a loop that cannot learn anything. The research question is what lies between them, and how to spend a finite information budget well.

02

Open questions

Budgeting

How should information released from a protected evaluator be measured, and what is a defensible allowance for a given release cadence?

Channel design

What form should feedback take — verdicts, aggregates, mechanisms — so that it remains useful for repair without disclosing the evaluation set?

Detecting exam-learning

What signals distinguish a candidate that genuinely improved from one that has adapted to the protected scenarios?

Refresh policy

When must protected scenarios be retired and regenerated, and what does that cost in comparability across versions?

SEAL is an active research direction. No results, benchmarks or guarantees are claimed on this page.

03

Why it matters here

FlightFoundry's release recommendation is only as trustworthy as the evaluation behind it. If the evaluation degrades quietly as the loop runs, every downstream claim degrades with it — and the failure mode is invisible precisely because the numbers keep improving.

That is why improvement and evaluation are separated in the product rather than in policy, and why the question of how much may leave the protected evaluator is treated as a research problem rather than a configuration setting.
